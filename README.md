## About the book

High-dimensional data are now routine across the physical, biological and engineering
sciences, and dimensionality reduction has become a standard step in extracting
interpretable structure from them. Yet no single technique is universally preferable: a
projection that is excellent for visualising clusters may be poor at preserving global
geometry or at supporting reconstruction.

This book replaces convention with evidence. Fourteen convex and nonconvex methods are
presented with their mathematical formulation, assumptions, computational cost and known
limitations, then assessed with thirteen normalised quality measures. The measures are
combined into four composite indices and weighted according to the analytical objective,
so that the choice of method follows from the purpose of the analysis. A robustness
analysis based on ±10% perturbation of the weights quantifies how stable each ranking is.

The framework is demonstrated on seven benchmark datasets from the UCI Machine Learning
Repository, covering sample sizes from 88 to 13,611 observations.

**Methods covered.** PCA · kernel PCA · L1-norm PCA · classical MDS · nonmetric MDS ·
Isomap · diffusion maps · DRR · FastICA · Hessian LLE · DrL · Fruchterman–Reingold ·
Kamada–Kawai · t-SNE.

**Objectives.** clustering · visualisation · reconstruction · machine-learning pipelines.
